## Differential Ideals And IBP

`Math.DifferentialEquation` takes a commutative complex algebra, a specified
family of derivations and an arbitrary set of polynomial equations. The ideal
is explicitly generated by every finite derivative word of every seed.
Minimality and derivative stability are proved. Commutation is needed only
to prove that the quotient derivatives commute; it is not assumed for the
ideal construction, Leibniz descent or EOM/IBP comparison.

The quotient projection is onto and its ring and linear kernels are exactly
the constructed ideal. The descended derivations use pinned mathlib's proved
surjective transport. The on-shell divergence range equals the image of the
off-shell divergence range. Consequently there are inverse source and
coefficient equivalences

\[
A/(J+\mathrm{Div}(A))\simeq
(A/J)/\mathrm{Div}(A/J).
\]

Here both outer quotients are linear quotients. Products of IBP classes are
not defined and divergences are never ideal-closed. Every finite-dimensional
off-shell subspace has a finite-dimensional quotient image. These images
need not form a direct sum; no interacting field-number grading or operator
count is inferred. Explicit ideal and derivative-covariance inputs give
algebra and IBP transport maps; generic covariance is not a physical premise
silently discharged by the construction.

The previous scalar linear ideal is recovered exactly when the seeds are
the linear forms of its full relation module. This connects the polynomial
construction to the retained source, without assuming that a nonlinear
quotient remains a free algebra on quotient generators.

## Associative Connections And Actual Matrix Jets

`Math.FormalConnection.Ordinary` supplies linear ordinary derivations on a
unital associative complex algebra, their Leibniz laws and their commutation.
For any connection components, define

\[
\nabla_i x=\partial_i x+[A_i,x],\qquad
F_{ij}=\partial_i A_j-\partial_j A_i+[A_i,A_j].
\]

Associativity and the supplied ordinary laws prove the covariant Leibniz
identity, curvature antisymmetry, the actual commutator
`[nabla_i,nabla_j] x = [F_ij,x]`, and cyclic covariant Bianchi. None of these
curvature identities is a hypothesis. Constant inverse conjugations preserve
the covariant derivative and curvature under explicit derivative-constant
conditions. Arbitrary local gauge transformations are not covered.

Entrywise derivatives instantiate this interface on finite matrices over the
existing jet algebra. Internal matrix noncommutativity is separate from the
commuting bosonic coefficient variables. A constant two-by-two connection
has curvature component one, showing that the construction includes genuinely
nonzero commutators rather than only the Abelian specialization.

\[

\qquad s=(1,-1,-1,-1).
\]

The equation entries generate the full differential ideal. All ordinary
