## Mathematical source and conventions

Peter Woit, *The Weyl Character Formula*, Math G4344, Spring 2012,
[author-hosted notes](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/LieGroups-2012/weylcharacter.pdf),
section 1 (character multiplicities), section 2, Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 2--3),
and section 3, Theorem 2 / Corollary 1 (PDF pp. 5--6).
The Weyl integral theorem requires a compact connected Lie group and a
maximal torus; finite-dimensional representations must be continuous.
A genuine torus character is Weyl invariant. Character orthogonality and the
Weyl denominator give the one-sided positive-root constant-term extraction
used here. This last formula is a specialization/derived consequence, not a
verbatim theorem quoted from the notes. The notes give an outline and refer
to Bröcker--tom Dieck for a detailed proof.

For independent simple roots alpha, beta, A2 uses alpha, beta, alpha+beta.
Multiplication by `(1-x^-root)` sends a coefficient function f(w) to
`f(w)-f(w+root)`; its value at zero is the constant term. The A2 formula has
six terms after the alpha+beta cancellation. SU2 uses one root of weight 2
in the usual diagonal-circle coordinate. Three independent SU2 factors are
needed for weak, left and right spin; the hypercharge circle has no roots.
The torus presentation in the initial interface represents the inverse
character, matching the already proved coefficient-Hom integral identity.
No arbitrary residual group is treated as a compact Lie group by default.
